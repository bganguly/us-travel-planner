US Travel & Motorcycle Planner — Travel Instructions, Rules, and Guidelines

=============================================================================
GENERAL US ROAD TRAVEL RULES
=============================================================================

Speed Limits:
- Interstate highways: 65–80 mph depending on state (Utah: 80 mph rural interstates; Arizona: 75 mph rural interstates)
- US highways and state routes: 55–65 mph unless posted
- National park roads: 35–45 mph unless posted; wildlife crossing zones may be 25 mph
- Residential and urban areas: 25–35 mph
- School zones: 15–25 mph when children are present

Motorcycle Rules (Utah):
- Helmet required for riders under 21; strongly recommended for all
- Eye protection required unless the motorcycle has a windscreen
- Lane splitting is not permitted in Utah
- Minimum liability insurance required
- Headlights must be on at all times

Motorcycle Rules (Arizona):
- Helmet required for riders under 18; optional but recommended for adults
- Eye protection required unless the motorcycle has a windscreen
- Lane splitting is not permitted in Arizona
- Minimum liability insurance required

=============================================================================
UTAH SCENIC BYWAY 12 (State Route 12) — Torrey to Panguitch
=============================================================================

Speed Limits:
- General posted limit: 55 mph on open sections
- The Hogback ridge segment (narrow cliffside): 35 mph; extreme caution advised — no guardrails on portions
- Boulder Mountain summit area: 45 mph; watch for ice October through May
- Within Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument corridor: 45 mph

Operating Hours:
- Route 12 is a public highway; open 24 hours year-round
- Calf Creek Recreation Area: dawn to dusk; fee required ($5 per vehicle, 2026 rate)
- Anasazi State Park Museum (Boulder, UT): 9 AM–5 PM daily; closed Sundays November–March

Key Restrictions:
- No overnight camping at highway pullouts; use designated campgrounds only
- Escalante Petrified Forest State Park: 7 AM–10 PM; $20/night camping fee
- Dogs must be on leash at all trailheads
- No off-road vehicle travel within National Monument without specific OHV permit
- Fuel available in Torrey, Boulder, Escalante, and Tropic — gaps of 30–60 miles; fill up at every opportunity
- No fuel available on the Boulder Mountain summit stretch (approx. 30 miles)

Weather Advisory:
- Route open year-round but Boulder Mountain segment (elevation 9,000 ft) may have snow and ice October–May
- Flash flood risk in canyon sections June–September; check forecast before entering slot canyons
- Recommended season for motorcycles: May through October

=============================================================================
ZION NATIONAL PARK — Zion Mount Carmel Highway (SR-9)
=============================================================================

Speed Limits:
- Zion Canyon Scenic Drive (inside park): 25 mph
- Zion Mount Carmel Highway (SR-9) approaching tunnel: 35 mph
- Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel area: follow ranger instructions; one-lane controlled passage for large vehicles

Operating Hours:
- Park entrance: open 24 hours; entrance fee applies at all times
- Entrance fee (2026): $35 per vehicle (7-day pass); $20 per motorcycle (7-day pass); America the Beautiful Annual Pass accepted
- Zion Canyon Scenic Drive (shuttle road): private vehicles prohibited March–November; park shuttle is free
- Visitor Center: 8 AM–5 PM (extended hours in peak season)
- Angels Landing permit required via lottery ($6 per person per day on top of park admission); apply at recreation.gov

Key Restrictions:
- Motorcycles may drive Zion Canyon Scenic Drive only December–February when private vehicles are permitted
- Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel escort fee applies for vehicles over 7 ft 10 in wide or 11 ft 4 in tall ($15 per vehicle; motorcycles exempt)
- Dogs prohibited on all trails inside Zion National Park; pets allowed on paved roads and in campgrounds
- No open campfires in backcountry; camp stoves only
- Backcountry permit required for overnight camping ($15 + $5/person/night)

Fuel:
- No fuel inside the park; nearest stations in Springdale (just outside south entrance) and at Mt. Carmel Junction (east entrance)

=============================================================================
GRAND CANYON NATIONAL PARK — South Rim & Desert View Drive (AZ-64)
=============================================================================

Speed Limits:
- Desert View Drive (AZ-64 inside park): 45 mph
- Grand Canyon Village area roads: 25 mph
- Mather Point and viewpoint access roads: 15 mph

Operating Hours:
- Park entrance: open 24 hours; entrance fee applies
- Entrance fee (2026): $35 per vehicle (7-day pass); $20 per motorcycle (7-day pass); America the Beautiful Annual Pass accepted
- Desert View Watchtower: 9 AM–5 PM daily (seasonal hours vary)
- Grand Canyon Visitor Center: 8 AM–5 PM daily
- Rim Trail: open 24 hours
- Hermit Road (west of Grand Canyon Village): closed to private vehicles March–November; free shuttle available; motorcycles prohibited on shuttle road during closure period

Key Restrictions:
- Mule trips require advance reservation (often booked 6–12 months ahead) at grandcanyonlodges.com
- No drone use anywhere in the park
- Dogs allowed on Rim Trail (paved) and in campgrounds; prohibited below the rim
- No hammock camping without specific site permit
- Fire restrictions: check current conditions at nps.gov/grca; open fires often restricted May–September
- No collecting rocks, fossils, or plants

Fuel:
- Desert View (east entrance area): gas station available April–October only
- Tusayan (south entrance): year-round fuel 5 miles from south rim entrance

=============================================================================
HISTORIC ROUTE 66 — Arizona (Kingman to Flagstaff via Oatman & Seligman)
=============================================================================

Speed Limits:
- Open desert sections between towns: 65 mph
- Oatman town center: 15 mph (wild burros frequently in road)
- Sitgreaves Pass (mountain switchbacks): 25–35 mph; sharp curves, limited visibility
- Seligman town: 25 mph

Operating Hours:
- Route 66 is a public highway; open 24 hours
- Oatman Hotel and gift shops: typically 8 AM–6 PM
- Hackberry General Store: 9 AM–5 PM (hours vary seasonally)
- Gold King Mine Museum (Jerome, AZ — nearby): 9 AM–5 PM

Key Restrictions:
- Oatman wild burros have right of way; stopping to feed is allowed but stay in vehicle or be cautious — burros can be aggressive
- Sitgreaves Pass: no passing zones on switchback sections; trailers and large RVs should avoid this route
- Cell service unreliable between Kingman and Oatman; download offline maps before departure
- Fuel available in Kingman, Oatman (limited), Seligman, and Ash Fork — plan accordingly; 90+ miles between reliable fuel stops possible

=============================================================================
MOTORCYCLE RENTAL GUIDELINES
=============================================================================

General Rules When Renting:
- Valid motorcycle endorsement (M class) on driver's license required for all rentals; out-of-state licenses accepted
- Minimum age: 21 for most rental companies (25 for some insurance tiers)
- Security deposit: typically $500–$1,500 on credit card (debit cards usually not accepted)
- Credit card insurance may cover rentals; verify with your card issuer before declining rental CDW
- Gear (helmet, jacket, gloves) available for rent or purchase at most major shops
- Inspect motorcycle thoroughly before departure; document any pre-existing damage in writing
- GPS/navigation mount: most rental bikes include; confirm before booking
- Return fuel policy: return full (most common) or return empty (less common); confirm at pickup

EagleRider Specific Rules:
- Free cancellation up to 48 hours before pickup
- Minimum 2-day rental for most locations
- One-way rentals available between select cities for additional fee
- Mileage: most plans include unlimited miles; verify at booking

NATIONAL PARK PASS OPTIONS
=============================================================================

America the Beautiful Annual Pass:
- Cost: $80/year (2026)
- Covers entrance fees at all national parks, monuments, and federal recreation areas
- Valid for the pass holder + 3 additional adults (children under 15 free)
- For motorcycles: covers the rider + one passenger
- Purchase: at any park entrance, recreation.gov, or USGS store online
- Recommended for any trip covering 3+ national parks (break-even at 3 parks at $20–$35/visit)

Zion Motorcycle Pass: $20 (7-day)
Grand Canyon Motorcycle Pass: $20 (7-day)
Bryce Canyon Motorcycle Pass: $20 (7-day)

=============================================================================
EMERGENCY AND SAFETY
=============================================================================

- Cell service is unreliable in canyon areas of Utah and Arizona; download offline maps (Google Maps, Gaia GPS) before departure
- Carry at least 2 liters of water per person at all times; refill at visitor centers
- Always tell someone your planned route and expected return time
- Utah and Arizona emergency roadside: call 511 (road conditions) or 911
- National Park emergency: 911 routes to park dispatch in most parks
- Nearest trauma centers: St. George, UT (for Zion/Byway 12 area); Flagstaff, AZ (for Grand Canyon/Route 66 area)
- Heat precautions: Grand Canyon and Route 66 temperatures exceed 110°F in July–August; ride early morning, rest midday
